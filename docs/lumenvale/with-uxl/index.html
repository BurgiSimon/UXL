18:20 Lights out

The minibus leaves the valley station at 17:30 and climbs 11 km of forest road to the observatory meadow. By the time you step out, the last light is behind Piz Lumen and your guide switches every lamp to red.

Your eyes need about 25 minutes. Pupils open from 2 to 7 mm and the rods in your retina, blind all day, take over. One white phone screen resets the clock, so we hand out red film for yours.

Stars above you 1,874

After full dark adaptation on a moonless night you can see stars down to magnitude 6: about 1,874 catalogue stars above the horizon at once.

19:10 Without a telescope

Before anyone touches an eyepiece, your guide walks you round the sky. Three things to find first:

  1. Polaris 433 light-years

    The one star on this plate that doesn't trail. Every other star turns around it, 15° an hour.

  2. The Andromeda Galaxy 2.5 million light-years

    A grey smudge high in the east, and the most distant thing you will ever see with your own eyes.

  3. The Pleiades 444 light-years

    Six or seven stars to most eyes. After 25 minutes in the dark, often nine.

20:05 Through the telescope

Inside the dome waits a 600 mm reflector on a fork mount. On autumn nights it shows Saturn and its moons, the Ring Nebula in Lyra, and Albireo, a double star of gold and blue.

Private telescope session +CHF 30

Twenty minutes at the 600 mm after the group tour, with only your party at the eyepiece and the guide steering to what you ask for. Two per night, per booking. Add it when you book.

21:25 The moon decides

At 21:25 your group rides down and the camera stays open until dawn. On any night, one thing decides how much of this you will see: a full moon lights the sky like a street lamp and drowns all but the brightest few hundred stars.

    06:30 Dawn. The camera closes.

    Every guest gets the plate.

    The morning after, we send you the night's finished exposure: every star that turned over the observatory while you were there.

    Book Sat 31 Oct or choose another night

    Getting here

    Meet at the Lumenvale valley station in Val Lumen. The PostBus from the railway station stops at the door and parking is free. The minibus up and back is included.

    What to bring

    Warm layers and a hat: it is often below zero at 2,140 m after dark. We lend down jackets, blankets and red torches. Children from 6 years.

    Questions

    nights@lumenvale.ch
    We answer within a day, and text you by 14:00 if clouds cancel your night.

    Lumenvale is a fictional observatory. Star positions from the Hipparcos and Yale Bright Star catalogues (via d3-celestial); moon phases, moonrise and twilight computed for 46.5° N, 8.05° E. Seat counts are illustrative. Back to the top
