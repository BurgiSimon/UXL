20:10 Thirty minutes in the dark

We meet at the last Postbus stop at dusk and walk the 600 m up to the dome without torches. The hut switches off its lights at 19:40. From then on, nobody brings a white light near the terrace, and your eyes do the slow part on their own.

Scroll slowly. The sky behind these words is following the same curve your eyes would.

30 min
How long the rods in your eyes need to reach full sensitivity. The cones are done in about 7. One look at a phone screen and you start over.
620 nm
Above this wavelength, deep red, the rods barely respond. That is why the paths, the guides' lamps and this page are lit in red.
6.9 mag
The faintest star you will see without help on a moonless night up here. In a city centre the limit is about 4, a twentieth of the stars.

21:00 The Moon decides

Only one thing undoes all of that. A bright Moon lights the whole sky and hides most of the faint stars, and no amount of waiting brings them back. Turn the dial through one lunar month and watch the sky behind it.

21:45 Without a telescope

Two hours in, the sky has turned 30° to the west and your eyes are fully adapted. Before the dome opens, the guides point out three things you can find again on your own.

  • The Milky Way through Cygnus

    Our own galaxy seen edge-on, straight overhead. Up here it is bright enough to show the Great Rift, a lane of dust that splits the band in two.

    Overhead, leaning west · 100 to 400 billion stars

  • The Andromeda Galaxy

    A soft oval of light beside the chain of Andromeda's stars. It is the most distant thing most people ever see with their own eyes.

    East, 55° up · 2.5 million light-years

  • The Pleiades

    A small dipper of blue stars rising in the north-east. Most people count six. Under this sky, count again.

    East-north-east, 25° up · 444 light-years

22:30 The slit opens

At half past ten the dome turns and the shutter rolls back. Our telescope is a 508 mm reflector. It gathers about 5,300 times more light than your dark-adapted eye, enough to show the dust lanes in the Andromeda Galaxy.

Everyone gets turns at the eyepiece: Saturn and its rings, the Ring Nebula in Lyra, a double star that is gold on one side and blue on the other.

Private session, CHF 30. Twenty minutes at the eyepiece for your party alone, with a guide steering to whatever you ask for. Add it when you book.

01:15 Lights on.

The shuttle leaves the Postbus stop at 01:30 and reaches the valley at 01:55. Most people are quiet on the way down.

Getting here

Postbus to Alp Lumen, then 600 m on foot, 80 m of climb. The stop is the meeting point. Parking in the valley only.

What to bring

Every warm layer you own. It is usually 10 °C colder than the valley, and below freezing from November. We lend blankets and boots.

Children

Welcome from age 6, with an adult. Under 16 they pay CHF 24. The walk is easy and the terrace has benches.
